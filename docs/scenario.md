# Enterprise Application Architecture Portfolio



## Synthetic-data notice



Meridian Property Services is fictional. All business entities,

applications, people, documents and data in this portfolio are synthetic.



This portfolio demonstrates architecture methods and working software.

It does not represent a real client engagement or production deployment.



## Business context



Meridian Property Services manages commercial properties through

two fictional business entities:



* Meridian North: manages commercial offices.
* Meridian South: manages retail properties.



The entities were brought together through an acquisition. They retain

separate operational responsibilities while sharing selected corporate

services.



## Current problems



* Overlapping applications support similar business activities.
* Some applications and integrations lack clearly assigned owners.
* Lease, billing and accounting records require manual reconciliation.
* Management lacks a consistent view of application dependencies.
* Information access must respect business-entity boundaries.



These are scenario assumptions to be explored through synthetic evidence.



## Portfolio objectives



1. Connect business capabilities and processes to applications.
2. Identify duplication, ownership gaps and unsupported capabilities.
3. Recommend a target application landscape and migration sequence.
4. Demonstrate reliable lease-to-billing integration.
5. Demonstrate multi-entity access controls.
6. Demonstrate permission-aware retrieval and controlled AI behavior.
7. Document architecture decisions and investment recommendations.



## Initial scope



The first project will model:



* Two business entities.
* Eight business capabilities.
* Three business processes.
* Ten applications.
* Six integrations.



Later projects will extend this landscape while preserving consistent

identifiers and terminology.



## Constraints



* Use synthetic data only.
* Begin with local development on Windows.
* Introduce additional services only when the demonstration needs them.
* Record cost estimates as assumptions rather than measured expenditure.
* Keep financial examples simplified and document their limitations.



## Initial success criteria



* Every application has a unique identifier.
* Applications are linked to the capabilities and processes they support.
* Application ownership is recorded or explicitly identified as missing.
* Integration references point to known applications.
* Inventory checks detect deliberately introduced defects.
* A reviewer can reproduce the checks from the repository instructions.



## Out of scope for the initial version



* Real customer or employee information.
* Production deployment.
* Actual accounting, tax or regulatory compliance.
* Claims of production-scale performance or availability.
